Drain Cleaning Checklist: One Slow Drain or the Whole House?

A slow sink and a backed-up main line are different jobs with different equipment. Work down this list before you call, and the plumber will hear what they need to know.

Call (380) 262-9173

Is only one fixture slow?

If one sink, tub or floor drain is slow and everything else is fine, the blockage is just past that fixture's trap. In a kitchen it is usually grease on the pipe wall; in a bathroom, hair bound up with soap scum. That is a local job, and a cable machine normally clears it.

Are several fixtures slow?

Run water at each fixture in turn and note which ones drain slowly. If several are affected, or the lowest drains back up when an upstairs fixture runs, the main line or the sewer lateral is involved.

Does anything gurgle?

Gurgling at more than one fixture, with water draining slowly but no hard clog, can mean a blocked vent stack: the pipe through the roof that lets air in behind the water. Look at the roof opening from the ground. Do not climb up.

Did water come up somewhere low?

Water surfacing in the lowest tub, shower or floor drain when the washer or toilet runs points to the main line. Common causes are roots at a clay joint, a bellied section holding water, scale in old cast iron or a failed lateral.

Have you done the safe checks?

Clear the visible stopper or strainer, check the trap under the sink and try a plain plunger on a single fixture. Find the outdoor cleanout cap and leave it closed. Skip chemical openers in a line that is already full.

Is it time to stop and call?

Yes, if water is rising in a tub, shower or floor drain, if there is a sewage smell indoors, or if the backup continues after you stop using water.
